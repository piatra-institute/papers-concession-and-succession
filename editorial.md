# Editorial record

Status: full read completed against the manuscript hashed in editorial-review.json.

## Contribution and reader

For anyone who has read that nonviolent campaigns work better and also that armed revolutions produce regimes that last, and has not noticed that the two findings are scoring different outcomes. The paper couples removal, durability and accountability in one model and locates the conditions under which a single decision moves them in different directions. Confidence is high for what the model implies, which is arithmetic under stated assumptions, and absent for anything empirical: no parameter here was estimated, and the paper says so four times because a reader skimming for a policy conclusion will find one that is not there.

## Material worth preserving

The narrow corner. The result that matters is not that escalation trades removal against accountability but that it improves the odds of removal only where an intact civilian campaign could strip at most 0.0475 of the apparatus's cooperation, 0.083 of the swept range. That the exception is small is what makes the paper consistent with the empirical baseline rather than a challenge to it, and the sentence saying the general finding is localized rather than overturned is the one that keeps the paper honest.

The differential-attrition null. Setting the two sensitivities equal returns the armed share to 0.1 exactly, which locates the composition shift in selectivity rather than in violence. A reader who suspects the second section is a machine for manufacturing radicalization can check it against its own null in one line.

The ensemble. Sixty-two of 600 draws is a small number and it would have been easy to report only the 62. Reporting that escalation helped in one draw in ten, and that in every one of those it cost accountability, is a stronger claim than a large effect at a chosen point would have been.

The concession from Simpson, Willer and Feinberg. The radical-flank experiments are the best evidence against this paper's conclusion and are given a paragraph that states their finding accurately before distinguishing the quantity they measure. A paper that had buried them would deserve less trust.

## Reverse outline

1. *Two findings that were never put in the same model* — states the two literatures, establishes with Geddes that removal is not the outcome anyone wants, and declares the model's status before any result appears. The declaration matters: everything after it is a property of stipulated functions, and a reader who misses this will misread the whole paper.
2. *What the incumbent is choosing between* — the three-option decision and its cost structure. Carries the attention result, which is the paper's most counterintuitive and least central finding, and the closed-form threshold, which is its most checkable.
3. *Composition changes without anyone changing their mind* — differential attrition, the closed form, and the null. Deliberately contains no decision by anyone, which is what makes it a mechanism rather than a story about radicalization.
4. *Three outcomes and three maxima* — the coupling. Carries the central result. The section is arranged so that the narrow corner is found before the divergence is displayed, because displaying the divergence first would invite the reader to assume the corner is large.
5. *Whether the divergence survives other parameters* — the ensemble, then the two strongest objections, answered rather than deflected.
6. *What is not being claimed* — four limits, three of which would strengthen the conclusion if repaired, and one, the differential sensitivity, which is the parameter everything in section 3 rests on and which nobody has measured.

## Changes the review made

The coupling was originally read at the boundary of the region where escalation helps removal. At that point the gain in removal probability is 0.0004 and the accountability cost is nearly total, which makes the trade-off look free and the argument look rigged. The focal point was moved to the institutional condition where escalating buys the *largest* gain in removal probability. That is the strongest available case for escalation, the gain there is 0.078 rather than 0.0004, and the conclusion survives it.

The model itself was rebuilt once during the work. In the first version escalation never raised the probability of removal anywhere, because repression destroyed armed capacity faster than arming built it and every optimum sat at zero escalation. That was a modelling artifact rather than a finding: escalation shifts a campaign's composition at the source, not only through differential attrition. Adding that channel produced an interior optimum and the divergence. The retuning also made the armed wing *less* differentially protected than in the first version, 2.1 times rather than 5.5, which is the more conservative assumption and costs the second section its most quotable claim, that the armed wing acquires a majority of surviving capacity. It does not; it reaches parity at 6.24 periods. The weaker claim is the one that is true of the model as it now stands.

Seventeen voice diagnostics were triaged and all seventeen rewritten: eleven em-dashes, two negations used as pivots, one inline contrastive, one item of house vocabulary and one spelled quantity. None was kept.

The source review found four works named in the brief as cornerstone literature — Acemoglu and Robinson, Kuran, Weinstein, Pearlman — sitting in references.yaml without a citation in the manuscript, along with four more that had been consulted and not placed. All eight were given sentences, and three of them state limits rather than support, which is where they honestly belong: what an armed wing becomes, whether a fragmented movement can hold a line, and whether a ruler can read the loyalty the model hands them.

## What a hostile reader should attack

The accountability function. It is a product of two terms, one rising in surviving civilian capacity and one falling in the armed share, and both the multiplicative form and the two coefficients are stipulated. The ensemble perturbs the coefficients and the divergence survives; it does not perturb the form, and a sufficiently different accountability function could weaken or reverse the third result. This is stated in the paper and is the weakest joint in it.

The two retention sensitivities. Everything in section 3 is a function of their ratio, and no empirical estimate exists. The paper reports the null and states the gap rather than covering it, but a reader who thinks the true ratio is close to one should conclude that section 3's magnitudes are near zero.

Whether the three outcomes are separable at all. The model treats durability and accountability as independent functions of composition, which is a simplification of two literatures that each contain the other's variable.
